## Scene intent

The reusable city page must give a visitor an honest, city-specific answer and route them to the appropriate residential or business tool without implying that every named city is served. It succeeds when one template can render only operator-approved city records, shared operational facts remain centralized, incomplete records cannot become public pages, and each published page contains genuinely useful local information rather than repeated search copy.

## Composition

Use a predominantly white canvas, deep navy type, and cyan sampled from the approved brand asset as the single dominant accent. Blue may remain reserved for established action styles, but it must not compete with cyan as the scene accent.

On desktop, use a 12-column layout:

- Breadcrumbs and the page introduction begin at the main content edge.
- The H1 and short introduction occupy approximately seven columns.
- A verified local summary occupies approximately five columns, aligned with the H1 rather than presented as a floating card.
- A full-width routing junction follows. Residential and business paths receive equal space when both are verified and actionable.
- If only one path is verified, use a single full-width route. Do not leave an empty branch or imply that the other service exists.
- Below the junction, place city details in ruled rows. Use approximately four columns for each row heading and eight for its verified content.
- Keep shared preparation and acceptance links visually secondary to the two primary paths.
- Do not use map tiles, location pins, glass cards, shadows, generic skyline imagery, or repeated SEO content boxes.

Use an approximately 56px to 72px responsive H1, 36px to 48px section headings, 18px to 20px body text, and 16px to 18px control labels. Use only the approved typeface and licensed weights.

On mobile, use one content column. Place the breadcrumb, H1, introduction, and verified local summary before the routing controls. When both paths exist, stack them as full-width links with a compact route junction between them. When only one path exists, show a single route with no decorative fork. Convert detail rows into heading-and-content groups without changing their source order. Primary targets should be 48px to 56px high.

The route graphic is an abstract service-routing device, not a geographic map. It may name the current city and verified outcomes, but its geometry must not suggest real distances, travel times, facility locations, or pickup tracking.

## States

The page contains three interactive groups: primary service-path links, directions and supporting-resource links, and breadcrumb links. The route graphic and operational status rows are not interactive.

| State | Primary service-path links | Directions and supporting-resource links | Breadcrumb links |
|---|---|---|---|
| Default | Show a visible label, support text, directional cue, boundary, and the related final route geometry. | Show descriptive linked text with an underline or equivalent non-color affordance. | Show linked ancestors as text links. Render the current city as unlinked text with `aria-current="page"` on its containing breadcrumb item when appropriate. |
| Hover | Increase boundary or line weight and advance the directional cue a few pixels. Do not rely on cyan alone. | Strengthen the underline and directional cue without moving surrounding layout. | Strengthen the underline without moving surrounding layout. |
| Focus | Show a high-contrast offset focus indicator around the complete target. Apply the same route emphasis used for hover. | Show the same consistent focus indicator and retain the underline. | Show the same consistent focus indicator and retain the underline. |
| Selected | Native cross-page links have no artificial selected state. If an approved same-page result exists, expose it through visible text and `aria-current="location"`. | No selected state is invented for native destination links. | The current city is not interactive and is distinguished by text and programmatic state, not color alone. |
| Loading | Native links have no artificial loading state. If genuine asynchronous routing delays a destination, retain the label, mark the destination region busy, and expose the loading status. | Use native navigation without loading UI unless a genuine asynchronous operation exists. | Use native navigation without loading UI. |
| Empty | A missing label or destination is a publication-blocking data error. Do not render an inert path. If one verified path remains, it expands to the available composition. If none remains, exclude the city page from production. | Omit an optional link when its verified target is absent. Do not render empty labels or `#` destinations. | Missing required breadcrumb destinations block publication. |
| Error | Preserve the real link destination when client enhancement fails. If a recoverable error is displayed, retain an operable link and add text plus an icon or boundary change. | Fall back to the native destination. Do not replace useful content with a toast. | Fall back to native destinations. |
| Reduced motion | Apply the complete emphasized or destination state immediately. Keep all route endpoints visible. | Apply underline and focus changes immediately. | Apply underline and focus changes immediately. |

City data has its own fail-closed states:

- Default: render a published, validated record with only verified paths and facts.
- Loading: core city content must be present in the initial HTML, so the public template has no initial loading screen.
- Empty: a city with no verified actionable path is not generated, linked, or included in the sitemap.
- Error: invalid references, unknown service status, missing provenance, or required missing copy fail the production build or server validation. They do not produce a partially populated public city page.
- Reduced motion: all content and final route geometry remain present without transition.

An explicitly verified unavailable service may appear as noninteractive status text. It must include plain language and an icon or structural label, not color alone. An unknown service status must not be presented as unavailable or available.

## Motion

- City route draw. Job: orient. Trigger: once after the H1, service summary, and available path links have painted. Duration: 600ms. Easing: `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`. Draw one route toward each verified path and settle completely. If there is one path, draw one route without a fork. Reduced-motion fallback: show the complete settled route on the first frame.
- Primary-path response. Job: orient. Trigger: pointer hover or keyboard focus on a primary service-path link. Duration: 150ms. Easing: `ease-out`. Increase the related route weight and advance its directional cue a few pixels. Reverse on pointer exit or blur in 120ms with `ease-in`. Reduced-motion fallback: apply the complete emphasized state immediately.
- Navigation handoff. Job: transition. Trigger: activation only when the approved routing architecture can preserve the chosen path on the destination page. Duration: no more than 400ms. Easing: `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)`. Never delay native navigation solely to finish the effect. Reduced-motion fallback: navigate immediately to the destination final state.

Do not add ambient motion, scroll-triggered city reveals, pulsing map points, moving vehicles, parallax, animated counters, smooth scrolling, or looping effects. Do not add a motion library. Plain CSS and a low-complexity SVG are sufficient.

## Accessibility

Use a breadcrumb `nav` with an accessible label, one H1, semantic sections with H2 headings, lists for grouped facts, and an `address` element only for a verified physical destination. Do not mark a city as a business location merely because it is served.

Render every navigation action as a real link with a valid destination in the initial HTML. Keep source and focus order as breadcrumb links, city introduction, residential path when present, business path when present, city-detail links, then inherited footer controls. Do not change visual order in a way that contradicts this sequence.

Treat abstract route geometry as decorative with an empty text alternative or `aria-hidden="true"` because adjacent headings and status text communicate every outcome. Do not put focusable elements inside the SVG. Informative city or facility photography requires a descriptive alternative based only on visible content.

Support Enter activation, visible focus at all breakpoints, 200 percent zoom, and reflow at 320 CSS pixels without clipping or horizontal scrolling. Each target must be at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, with 48px to 56px preferred for primary mobile actions.

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast: at least 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text, and 3:1 against adjacent colors for focus indicators, meaningful route strokes, and boundaries required to identify controls. Do not place small text in cyan unless the sampled production value passes contrast.

Communicate available, unavailable, contact-first, selected, and error states through text, iconography, boundary or line changes, and programmatic state in addition to color. Do not announce decorative route changes. Native navigation needs no live announcement. Announce only genuine asynchronous loading or errors through a concise status region without moving focus unexpectedly.

Directions links should use normal same-tab navigation unless the site-wide policy explicitly requires otherwise. If a new tab is used, disclose that behavior in the accessible name and visible context.

## Performance

Render the breadcrumb, H1, local summary, verified service paths, city details, and complete final route in the initial HTML without waiting for application JavaScript or animation code. Prefer static generation or server rendering for published city records. A chosen stack that requires client fetching for the core city answer must be raised under Open questions before implementation.

Treat the text H1 as the likely Largest Contentful Paint element. Reserve dimensions for the route SVG and any approved image. Use responsive modern image formats and lazy-load any authentic below-the-fold photography. Do not ship city photography until a real, approved asset exists.

Load only approved font weights and provide durable system fallbacks. Shared operational data must resolve during the build or server render so client-side hydration is not required to display fees, hours, location information, or service status.

Deliberately do not ship a map library, third-party map embed, geolocation prompt, map tiles, WebGL, 3D assets, autoplay video, motion library, client-side city search, generic stock photography, or analytics that block rendering. Directions may use a normal verified external URL without embedding a map.

Generate only validated public city routes. Draft and incomplete records must not enter production output, internal navigation, metadata feeds, or the sitemap.

## Data and facts required

Use one structured city collection as the source for page identity and city-specific content. Shared operational facts must live in separate canonical collections and be referenced by stable identifiers.

| Data | Required structure | Required source and publication rule |
|---|---|---|
| City identity | Stable identifier, URL slug, display name, administrative area, canonical path, publication state | Operator-approved city registry. A city name in the vision is not proof of service coverage. |
| Unique page metadata | Approved title, meta description, local summary, and optional social description | Operator or approved content owner. Copy must be unique and supported by the city record. |
| Service paths | Audience, status of `available`, `unavailable`, `contact_required`, or `unknown`, public summary, action label, action destination, source reference, verification date | Maintained operator-approved service-area and service-definition records. `unknown` is never publishable as a public status. |
| Residential applicability | Whether residential drop-off is offered to or relevant for the city | Operator-approved residential service policy. Do not infer it from proximity. |
| Business applicability | Whether business pickup is offered in the city and any applicable boundary or qualification | Operator-approved commercial service-area policy. Do not infer it from the East Valley brand description. |
| Drop-off destination | Reference to a canonical location record | Operator-maintained location source. Do not copy the address into each city record. |
| Address and directions | Physical address, destination label, and approved directions URL | Operator-maintained location source. Do not derive travel time, distance, or route claims. |
| Hours and closures | Reference to the canonical schedule, including time zone and maintained closure handling | Operator-maintained hours source. Do not place free-form duplicated hours in city records. |
| Accepted items, conditions, and fees | References to the canonical acceptance and fee policies or tool destination | Operator-approved maintained policy. Do not copy values into city prose. |
| Preparation guidance | References to canonical preparation resources plus any verified city-specific exception | Operator-approved preparation policy. |
| Contact-first path | Approved contact route and public explanation | Operator-approved contact workflow. Do not render this status without an operable contact destination. |
| City-specific detail | One or more genuinely useful local content blocks with provenance | Operator or approved content owner. Repeated keyword substitutions do not qualify. |
| Related cities | Explicit ordered references to other published city records | Operator-approved editorial relationship. Do not calculate or imply geographic proximity without approved data. |
| Photography | Asset reference, dimensions, crop guidance, alternative text, rights status | Approved authentic EVER asset library. |
| Fact provenance | Source identifier, approving owner, verified date, and review status for every operational field | Maintained operator record. The operator must define review ownership and staleness policy. |
| Brand assets | Approved wordmark, route usage guidance, exact colors, typeface files, licensed weights, and fallbacks | Operator-approved production brand package. |
| Routing architecture | City route pattern, local-page index route, acceptance destination, business intake destination, and error destination | Operator-approved site architecture after the web stack is chosen. |
| Legal entity metadata | Public business name, canonical site URL, and any physical location or service-area facts intended for machine-readable markup | Operator-approved legal and operational source. |

The data validator must enforce these publication rules:

- A published city needs a verified identity, unique metadata, a unique local summary, provenance, and at least one verified actionable service path.
- Every service path must resolve to a maintained source record and valid destination.
- Referenced locations, schedules, policies, resources, and related cities must exist and be eligible for publication.
- An unavailable status needs operator-approved public wording. A contact-required status also needs a valid contact destination.
- Unknown status values, unresolved references, placeholder tokens, and stale records under the operator-defined policy block publication.
- A city record must not duplicate canonical fees, hours, addresses, accepted-item rules, or preparation instructions.
- Draft records and synthetic test fixtures must be excluded from public routes, navigation, structured metadata, and sitemaps.
- Page metadata and JSON-LD must be derived only from facts visible on the page or from the approved organization record.
- Use `WebPage` and `BreadcrumbList` markup when their fields are valid.
- Add `LocalBusiness`, `PostalAddress`, or `areaServed` properties only when the operator has supplied and approved those exact facts.
- Never represent every city page as a separate physical business location.
- Do not emit ratings, reviews, prices, hours, certifications, founding details, or service-area claims without matching approved records.

## Build steps

1. Record the operator's web-stack decision, city URL pattern, local-page index destination, acceptance destination, business intake destination, and production data location before creating the template.
2. Obtain and configure the approved brand colors, typeface files and weights, wordmark, and route-geometry guidance before making layout decisions dependent on their metrics.
3. Define the city-record contract, referenced shared-record contracts, service-status vocabulary, provenance fields, publication state, and operator-defined fact-review fields.
4. Build validation that rejects unknown public service states, unresolved references, missing unique content, missing provenance, placeholder tokens, invalid destinations, and records with no verified actionable path.
5. Add clearly synthetic test fixtures outside the production content collection. Ensure their identifiers contain an unmistakable test marker and that production generation cannot include them.
6. Build the city-record resolver so locations, hours, acceptance rules, fees, preparation guidance, and destinations come from canonical shared records instead of copied city values.
7. Build route generation for validated published records only. Exclude drafts, incomplete records, and test fixtures from routes, navigation, metadata feeds, and the sitemap.
8. Build the semantic page structure with breadcrumbs, one H1, local summary, verified service paths, city-detail sections, and supporting links. Ensure all destinations work without client JavaScript.
9. Build the desktop 12-column composition, responsive type scale, ruled detail rows, single-path layout, and two-path routing junction.
10. Build the mobile single-column composition, including the compact junction between two full-width paths and the unforked layout when only one path exists.
11. Add the low-complexity decorative route SVG with final geometry present before enhancement and path count derived only from verified service records.
12. Build default, hover, focus, selected, loading, empty, error, and reduced-motion behavior for each link group and the fail-closed page-data states.
13. Add the one-time route draw and direct-input response in CSS. Add no ambient motion or library dependency.
14. Generate unique canonical metadata, sitemap entries, breadcrumbs, and permitted JSON-LD from the validated visible record. Prevent physical-location and service-area markup from being inferred.
15. Add automated checks for validation failures, shared-record resolution, route generation, placeholder exclusion, metadata parity, single-path rendering, two-path rendering, and production exclusion of test fixtures.
16. Test a validated synthetic page and each failure state with JavaScript disabled, keyboard-only input, a screen reader, reduced motion, 200 percent zoom, 320 CSS pixel reflow, slow network conditions, and CPU throttling.
17. Capture paused desktop and mobile screenshots of the synthetic page, confirm that the path hierarchy and local answer remain clear without motion, and store the required runtime observations in the sibling evidence file.
18. Add real city records only after the operator provides and approves every required fact. Do not publish any city named in the vision solely because it appears there.

## Open questions

- What URL pattern should city pages use, and what is the canonical route for the local-page index?
- The vision names Gilbert, Mesa, Chandler, Queen Creek, Tempe, and San Tan Valley as map concepts, but does not verify coverage. Which cities, if any, have operator-approved service records?
- Does the operator want the template and data contract built with no public city pages until facts arrive, or should work wait for the first approved city record?
- What minimum city-specific information does the operator consider genuinely useful enough to authorize publication?
- Is residential drop-off available and relevant for each approved city?
- Is business pickup available for each approved city, and what boundaries or qualifications apply?
- What are the canonical destinations for the acceptance tool, business pickup intake, preparation guidance, local-page index, and error recovery link?
- What location, address, hours, directions, closure, acceptance, fee, and preparation sources will act as the maintained records?
- What public wording should represent verified unavailable and contact-required service states?
- Who owns approval and ongoing review of city facts?
- What review interval or expiration rule should make operational records stale and block publication?
- Which legal entity, physical location, and service-area facts may appear in machine-readable metadata?
- Which production typeface, wordmark, color values, and route-geometry usage rules are approved?
- Are authentic city-specific or facility photographs available, with publication rights and approved alternative text?
- Should related city links be manually curated, or omitted until the operator can verify that each relationship is useful?
- The vision encourages useful search-focused city pages but no approved city-specific operational data exists in the repository. This prevents public page creation until the operator supplies that data.
- If the selected stack cannot provide core city content in the initial HTML, should the architecture change? A client-only loading experience would conflict with the vision's requirement that the first useful answer render without animation code.
